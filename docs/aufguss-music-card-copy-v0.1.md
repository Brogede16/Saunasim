# Sauna Sim Aufguss Music Card Copy v0.1

## Purpose

This is the first English player-facing card-copy library for the ten approved music directions. Music is represented by original short loops and profiles, never licensed songs. Each card explains the musical role in a Gus and a concrete risk when it is paired with the wrong heat, performance or promise.

## Silence & Natural Sound

**About**  
No soundtrack beyond water, wind, wood and the room itself. This direction lets heat, scent and towelwork become the main event.

**Good to know**  
Use it for Classic Ritual, Quiet Recovery or a small intimate Gus. Do not choose it for a program built around Rhythmic Flow or a large social show; those styles need a shared pulse.

## Ambient

**About**  
Slow, spacious sound with little pressure to follow a beat. Ambient supports measured heat and gives quieter materials time to register.

**Good to know**  
Use it with Gentle Build, Fir, Sage, Lavender, Green Tea or Hinoki. Avoid using it as the only energy source for a hard High-Heat Finale; the music and heat will pull in different directions.

## Acoustic & Folk

**About**  
Warm instruments, clear rhythm and a human scale. Acoustic & Folk makes a traditional or social Gus feel shared without turning it into a show.

**Good to know**  
Use it with birch, spruce, woods, herbs, Beer & Hops or a water-side ritual. Do not pair it with a heavily choreographed electronic show unless the contrast is part of a named idea.

## Classical & Instrumental

**About**  
Structured melodies without a dominant vocal hook. Classical & Instrumental gives a program clear pacing and supports precise towelwork.

**Good to know**  
Use it with Progressive Rounds, Cedarwood, Hinoki or a refined premium ritual. Avoid using it with deliberately messy Event Essences unless the performance gives the contrast a clear story.

## Soul & Jazz

**About**  
Warm groove, relaxed movement and an evening character. Soul & Jazz makes a social or premium Gus feel welcoming rather than loud.

**Good to know**  
Use it with Orange, Cardamom, Cedarwood or a rest-deck finish. Do not use it for a fast, high-pressure finale without another rhythmic element; the music is relaxed by design.

## Pop & Disco

**About**  
Familiar hooks and clear shared energy. Pop & Disco can make a Gus feel accessible, playful and easy to join.

**Good to know**  
Use it with Social Energy, citrus, Dark Chocolate & Mint or a visible social setting. Avoid pairing it with a solemn Myrrh ritual or very quiet recovery promise; guests will receive mixed signals.

## Electronic & Techno

**About**  
Clean pulse, repetition and forward movement. Electronic & Techno gives Rhythmic Flow a beat the Master can visibly work with.

**Good to know**  
Use it with Rhythmic Pulses, an Energetic Master, urban/industrial energy or a controlled High-Heat Finale. Do not add it to Gentle Build and Lavender recovery without an explicit reason for the contrast.

## Rock & Metal

**About**  
Hard edges and a strong pulse. Rock & Metal can turn heat into a shared event and give a Gus a raw, memorable identity.

**Good to know**  
Use it with Bold heat, Rhythmic Flow, Chilli Orange, Beer & Hops, Whisky or a confident event setting. Avoid using it as background to a quiet, delicate recovery Gus; it can overpower the program's calmer choices.

## Ritual Percussion

**About**  
Repetition, hand-played rhythm and a physical sense of pace. Ritual Percussion can make a simple Gus feel focused and ceremonial.

**Good to know**  
Use it with outdoor ritual, vihta, Myrrh, woods or a deliberate breathing-space between rounds. Do not stack it with fast electronic music or complex show choreography; the Gus will have two competing rhythms.

## Film Scores

**About**  
Music that builds scenes and transitions. Film Scores give a Story-led Performance a clear beginning, middle and ending.

**Good to know**  
Use it with a named Show Journey, progressive heat and materials that change role from round to round. Do not choose it for a simple short routine Gus; the music promises more development than the program has time to deliver.

## Copy Review Rules

- Music descriptions must never imply that a genre belongs to a demographic group.
- They may describe setting or energy, but never hard-lock a genre to a location.
- Music is original game audio; copy cannot refer to real artists, songs or playlists.
- Before implementation, create 2-4 approved paraphrases for each card while preserving this version as the tone reference.
