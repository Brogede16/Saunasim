# Sauna Sim: Feedback and Improvement Map v0.1

Last updated: August 23, 2026

## Rule

Guest feedback is the player's main way to learn the hidden market. Every player-facing criticism must be either:

- traceable to a concrete decision or operational condition, and
- answerable by at least one meaningful player action.

Do not surface complaints about a permanent site property unless the report also identifies a possible adaptation. The player may not be able to move a canal or create a forest, but they can change the promise, program, service, capacity, marketing or price.

## Feedback Cadence

### During operation

Show only a few lightweight signals:

- an entrance queue
- a visibly quiet or busy exterior
- a short guest thought or small icon
- program attendance

The player should not need to read a stream of pop-ups.

### Weekly review

Show 1-3 concrete observations, selected for usefulness rather than completeness. Each observation names the cause and points toward a relevant improvement area.

### Monthly review

Show durable patterns:

- retention direction
- strongest/weakest visit motivation served
- revenue mix
- venue reputation and Standing movement
- whether brand awareness is changing the audience

## Guest Feedback to Player Action

| Guest signal | Underlying possible cause | Player can respond by | Not acceptable as feedback if |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| "The evening queue took the edge off." | Session demand exceeds changing, sauna or entry throughput | Improve changing capacity, add sauna capacity, adjust program frequency, add staff | The player has no plausible capacity response |
| "The ritual was beautiful, but too intense for me." | Heat, duration or performance style misses some preferences | Change program recipe, offer a gentler alternative, assign a better-fitting master | It reads as a hidden numerical penalty |
| "The session was worth the price." | Price and delivered experience align | Keep price, strengthen the program, use this in marketing | There is no visible reason for value |
| "Nice place, but I brought my own towel and did not need much else." | Guest has low add-on need; service mix is not their priority | Treat as valid behaviour; do not force every guest to buy extras | The game labels normal guest choice as failure |
| "I would stay longer if there was somewhere to cool down." | Recovery/cold-water need is unmet | Add plunge, bathing bridge, water station, deck or recovery feature | The site makes any recovery option impossible |
| "The team seemed rushed." | Too little staff or high workload | Hire staff, reduce program frequency, improve capacity, change opening-hours approach | Workload has no operational remedy |
| "I did not know this place was here." | Low awareness or understated exterior | Add sign/program frame, local campaign, opening event, clearer concept | Marketing is the only possible response forever |
| "I came for the lake, and the whole visit delivered." | Natural setting plus experience align | Preserve the experience, market the destination, build water-facing facilities | Natural water is treated as an automatic win |
| "I came once, but it is not part of my routine." | Weak fit for convenience visitors or weak return reason | Change price, improve reliable service, create routine-friendly program, target a different guest mix | The report offers no direction |
| "The show was fun, but not what I was looking for tonight." | Good program, wrong motivation for this guest | Keep it for its audience; offer a second program or market it more specifically | One guest is treated as universal proof the program failed |

## The Four Diagnostic Categories

Every major weekly observation belongs to one category. This prevents unclear feedback.

| Category | Meaning | Typical remedy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fit | The concept is not serving the guests currently attracted | Adapt program, price, marketing or service mix |
| Execution | A good idea was delivered poorly | Change staff assignment, staff level, workload or program setup |
| Capacity | Demand exists but the venue cannot carry it | Build, staff, reduce pressure or change frequency |
| Awareness | The right guests do not yet know or understand the venue | Improve signage, program identity, reputation or marketing |

## Player-Psychology Constraints

### Criticism must preserve agency

The player should think: "I know what to try next," not "the game has decided this location is bad."

### Mixed feedback is good

A guest can like an Aufguss but dislike the queue. This makes improvement layered and prevents one score from flattening the venue into success/failure.

### Do not demand universal approval

The goal is a coherent, successful concept, not a sauna every guest loves. A program may deliberately serve one motivation extremely well.

### Prioritise patterns over noise

One comment is colour. Repeated comments, retention signals and weekly patterns are diagnosis.

## Weekly Review Example

```text
WEEK 12: NORTHSIDE DEPOT

The good
"Birch After Work" generated strong repeat visits from routine guests.

The pressure
Your two evening sessions sold out, but the entrance queue grew after 18:00.

Try next
Improve changing capacity, add a host, or reduce late-session pressure.

The opportunity
Social guests stayed longer when the outdoor bench terrace was open.
```

This report tells the player what worked, what is limiting it, and which decision space to explore next without revealing the formula.
