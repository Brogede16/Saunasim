# Sauna Sim Cross-Venue Balance Protocol v0.1

## Purpose

This protocol defines how Sauna Sim is balanced once every offer, asset and program has a provisional data card. It prevents one location, water route, capacity size, staff role or loan product from becoming the obvious universal answer.

It is a test method, not a final price table. Dollar values remain provisional until the scenarios run against a deterministic simulation.

## Balance Principle

Every viable venue family must offer three kinds of success:

1. A modest, affordable `Normal` route that can sustain the venue.
2. A differentiated `Rare` route that rewards learning its physical strengths.
3. At least one potential `Iconic` route whose investment, staff and operational pressure are comparable to other families' strong routes.

Comparable does not mean identical attendance, price, capacity, profit or art scope. It means no family is structurally unable to reach a rewarding concept, and no family gets all of capacity, low cost, premium acceptance, easy flow and destination pull.

## Scenario Bands

Run each approved venue seed through these bands.

| Band | State | What it tests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `starter` | Existing base, basic conversion, owner/one appropriate Master, one modest Gus | Can a careful player stay alive without premature borrowing? |
| `focused` | One targeted upgrade solving an observed pressure | Does the upgrade create a specific useful improvement rather than a flat income boost? |
| `developed` | Two or three coherent upgrades, suitable staff and a matching program | Does a venue reach a compelling differentiated route at reasonable operating complexity? |
| `ambitious` | High-cost room/facility or premium event route with financing pressure | Is the upside real but dependent on execution, demand, recovery and debt service? |
| `stressed` | One visible bottleneck: queue, workload, condition, price mismatch or recovery congestion | Is the failure legible and answerable by more than one player decision? |

## Required Measures

All measures are simulation-facing. The player sees their consequences through money, scene activity and feedback rather than a score sheet.

| Measure | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Net operating result | Basic viability after wages, venue obligations, use-driven costs and materials. |
| Margin per Master-minute | Prevents a tiny premium room or one high-priced program from dominating. |
| Margin per physical capacity channel | Separates entry, sauna, special Gus, cold/warm recovery, rest and shop bottlenecks. |
| Admission conversion | Tests price, opening rhythm, awareness and visible promise. |
| Program fill and occupancy | Tests whether session scale matches its intended intimacy/social/show promise. |
| Queue/abandonment count | Ensures demand creates a visible, actionable pressure rather than invisible lost income. |
| Return/regular direction | Tests whether quality and recovery create durable value without snowballing. |
| Debt service coverage | Tests whether a loan is a meaningful commitment but not an arbitrary trap. |
| Condition/technician load | Tests whether maintenance stays a light planning layer. |
| Venue Fit benefit | Tests that physical/market fit creates bounded targeting, value and retention only. |

## Mandatory Comparisons

### 1. Water Versus Non-Water

Compare a natural-water route against a comparable rooftop, industrial, rural or urban built-recovery route.

- Water may improve destination/recovery identity and price acceptance.
- It may not create free capacity, free execution quality or a flat attendance multiplier.
- Non-water venues must have viable high-fit routes through program quality, shelter, service, controlled cold/warm recovery, access or visible promise.

### 2. Small Versus Large Program Scale

Compare intimate 8-12 seat, mid-scale 16-30 seat and larger 40+ seat formats.

- Small programs may charge more only with genuine intimacy/quality/fit.
- Large programs may earn through accessible price and shared experience, but pay more in flow, recovery, staffing and maintenance.
- Neither wins solely because of a seat count.

### 3. Low-Cost Versus Premium Venue

Compare tent/container/depot routes with pavilion/rooftop/estate/kursted routes.

- Low-cost venues need an honest profitable niche and modest expansion path.
- Premium venues need enough delivery, service and recovery to justify acquisition and price.
- Higher purchase price may be better, but never automatically safer or more profitable.

### 4. Program Intent Coverage

For each venue family, test at least one credible `Classic Ritual`, `Quiet Recovery`, `Social Energy` or `Show Journey` direction as appropriate. No venue must support every intent equally, but every location/building pair needs more than one way to be interesting.

### 5. Staff and Automation

Compare owner-only, first Master, Host-supported, multiple-Master and Operations-Manager chain states.

- A Master must earn their wage when used well.
- A Host must solve flow/service pressure, not be a generic profit bonus.
- Technicians must reduce repair risk without becoming a constant click burden.
- Operations Managers must improve visible procurement/operations/marketing terms but leave programs, prices, venues, loans and projects to the player.

### 6. Finance and Risk

Test cash-funded, small-loan and standard-loan versions of the same development direction.

- Loans must accelerate a coherent plan but expose an understandable repayment burden.
- Negative cash must present factual choices, not a false forecast or unavoidable surprise.
- A strong existing venue can increase chain borrowing room, but cannot make new sites locally successful by itself.

## Pass Conditions

A scenario passes only when all statements are true:

1. The result can be explained in player language by Fit, Execution, Capacity or Awareness.
2. At least one meaningful player response exists for any pressure.
3. A high result comes from a combination of physical support, program quality, market/value fit and staffing, not one hidden multiplier.
4. The route remains compatible with the building's realistic capacity envelope.
5. The same rule produces the same result online and after equivalent offline elapsed time.
6. No demographic trait, appearance or name enters the outcome calculation.

## Failure Patterns To Reject

- A water location makes more money before any matching program/recovery route is built.
- A Program Sauna is profitable even when empty or without a suitable Master/program.
- A shop outperforms the sauna business regardless of flow or guest fit.
- A small high-price session dominates larger venues without a strong intimacy/value explanation.
- A high Brand Value/rank creates enough demand to hide queues, price mismatch or weak local fit.
- A technician/manager makes all meaningful decisions automatic.
- A trend reduces an otherwise healthy program below its baseline.
- A repair occurs so frequently that it replaces programming and venue decisions as the main play loop.

## Execution Order

1. Freeze the Canal reference envelope and tests.
2. Test the six first-offer eligible seeds at `starter` and `focused` bands.
3. Test every building seed at `developed` band.
4. Test later premium seeds at `ambitious` and `stressed` bands.
5. Only then tune chain Brand Value, rank, trends and multiplayer-reference inputs.

## Deliverables From The Balance Pass

- A versioned scenario table with inputs, outcomes and intended player feedback.
- A list of changed numbers with reason, rather than undocumented quiet tuning.
- Automated deterministic tests for Canal plus representative non-water, water, low-cost and premium cases.
- A short player-facing balance note explaining the intended strategic differences between venue families.
