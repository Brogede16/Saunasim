# Sauna Sim: First Sites and Upgrades v0.1

Last updated: August 23, 2026

## Purpose

This is the proposed first content package for the game. It gives the player three meaningfully different but financially viable starting sites, then a small upgrade catalogue that demonstrates the core systems:

- location and concept fit
- capacity versus demand
- visible venue growth
- natural water and outdoor facilities
- marketing and first impression
- in-venue experience and service

Nothing here requires a full interior cutaway or a unique art pipeline per site. These are content proposals until approved in the decision log.

## 1. Content Rules

### Every site is viable

The three starting offers have different strengths, but none is a trap. Each can reach a healthy first venue through more than one concept.

### Each upgrade has one primary job

An upgrade can have secondary effects, but its core purpose must be obvious. Examples: solve a capacity bottleneck, create a cold-water experience, increase service spend, or make a venue more visible.

### Site changes the version, not the value judgement

The same underlying upgrade can appear differently by site:

- a city cold plunge becomes a compact roof facility
- a waterfront cold-water experience becomes a bathing bridge
- a nature site gets a timber deck and outdoor plunge

These are feasibility, cost and visual differences. They should not imply that one style is inherently correct.

## 2. The Three Starting Site Offers

All three offers are existing buildings and fit inside the same realistic starting-capital band. Prices below are intentionally not set yet; use exact currency in the game once the balance model exists.

### A. Canal Workshop

**Visible setting:** A narrow former repair workshop beside a city canal. Brick facade, modest paved frontage, water at the edge, evening lights and passing pedestrians.

**Property character:** Medium footprint, high rent, high potential evening flow, direct but constrained water access.

**Player-facing clues:**

- "The canal stays active long after offices close."
- "Space is tight, but the water draws attention."
- "Local guests expect the experience to feel deliberate."

**Natural early strengths:**

- visible city foot traffic
- strong potential for a named evening Aufguss
- direct path to a compact bathing bridge

**Natural pressures:**

- limited changing and outdoor capacity
- high recurring cost
- expansion must be compact, vertical or water-facing

**Visual evolution:** A brick building gains warm signage, a canal deck, bathing bridge, lit glass entrance, then possibly a compact roof facility.

**Viable concepts:** A focused evening ritual sauna, a social city sauna, or a premium small-scale concept.

### B. Northside Depot

**Visible setting:** A low former depot in a mixed city district. Broad facade, a paved forecourt, space behind the building, bicycles and light road traffic.

**Property character:** Larger basic footprint, medium recurring cost, moderate all-day local flow, no natural water.

**Player-facing clues:**

- "The building has room to become something distinctive."
- "The district is practical, varied and growing."
- "Guests care about a smooth visit as much as a spectacle."

**Natural early strengths:**

- easiest indoor capacity and service expansion
- broad audience and reliable daily traffic
- room for a terrace, shop, or later roof facility

**Natural pressures:**

- must create its own cold-water draw
- less immediate destination romance than water or nature sites
- concept needs more visible identity to stand out

**Visual evolution:** A simple industrial shell gains a new front sign, planted forecourt, side extension, terrace or a visually ambitious roof plunge.

**Viable concepts:** An accessible local sauna, a well-run program-led sauna, or an ambitious urban destination built over time.

### C. Pine Lake Pavilion

**Visible setting:** A modest existing pavilion at a forest lake. Timber facade, a small deck, trees, reeds and direct lake access.

**Property character:** Lower recurring cost, lower casual walk-in flow, strong destination and natural-water potential.

**Player-facing clues:**

- "People come here when they want to stay awhile."
- "The lake is the headline, but it takes effort to bring people here."
- "A calm, cared-for experience travels by word of mouth."

**Natural early strengths:**

- low-cost, high-character bathing bridge
- strong outdoor and relaxation appeal
- space for later expansion

**Natural pressures:**

- demand must be built through concept, reputation or marketing
- lower spontaneous walk-in traffic
- a large program schedule can outgrow local demand before brand awareness grows

**Visual evolution:** The pavilion gains a longer bathing bridge, lakeside deck, fire area, small glass extension and eventually a destination-scale outdoor sauna.

**Viable concepts:** A calm traditional sauna, a high-end destination experience, or a highly distinctive ritual-focused concept.

## 3. Suggested Starting State

Each first venue opens with the same invisible basic functional foundation:

- a modest sauna
- minimum changing and shower capacity
- simple reception/entry handling
- basic guest service

The exterior shows only the building and setting. The phone tells the player which internal functions are under pressure.

The first decision is not a room-layout editor. It is a choice of the first improvement and how the venue should present itself.

## 4. Upgrade Catalogue v0.1

### 4.1 Immediate / Small Upgrades

| Upgrade | Primary job | Visible result | Site adaptation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Exterior Sign and Program Frame | Awareness and first impression | Sign, poster frame, lit entrance | Brick wall, forecourt sign or timber noticeboard |
| Water Station | Guest comfort and dwell quality | Small exterior water point only when appropriate; otherwise phone-led interior change | Same mechanics, low visual cost |
| Towel Service | Convenience and service spend | Subtle entry canopy, towel basket or service sign | Same mechanics, small facade layer |
| Better Changing Facilities | Reduce entry/exit bottleneck | Small side section or cleaner entrance treatment | Visual may be minimal; core effect is interior capacity |
| Outdoor Bench Terrace | Waiting comfort and exterior appeal | Benches, deck or paved seating | Canal edge, depot forecourt or lakeside deck |

### 4.2 Medium / Identity Upgrades

| Upgrade | Primary job | Visible result | Site adaptation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reception Shop | Optional guest spend and service identity | Window counter, small shop sign, facade activity | Works at all sites |
| Sauna Extension | More session capacity and stronger silhouette | Side volume, taller chimney, extended roofline | Building-family-specific facade layer |
| Atmospheric Lighting | Strengthen program mood and evening first impression | Warm lamps, facade light, subtle evening glow | Same reusable light language |
| Premium Program Kit | Enables costly high-end Aufguss running choices | Program banner/emblem and small exterior cues | Interior mechanics; reused effects during Aufguss |
| Outdoor Relaxation Deck | Improves dwell time and destination appeal | Deck, loungers, planting, fire bowl where appropriate | City roof/forecourt, canal deck, lake deck |

### 4.3 Major / Location-Defining Upgrades

| Upgrade | Primary job | Visible result | Site adaptation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cold Plunge Facility | Strong post-sauna experience and a new capacity point | Tank, small pool or roof plunge | Roof plunge in dense city; deck tank at depot; lakeside plunge at pavilion |
| Bathing Bridge | Direct natural-water ritual and major visual identity | Pier, steps and water access | Only water-adjacent sites; canal and lake versions |
| Glass or Timber Extension | Capacity, appeal and premium identity | New visible wing with changed silhouette | Glass in city/canal, timber/glass at lake, mixed at depot |
| Destination Outdoor Sauna | Large capacity and destination pull | Separate small exterior sauna building | Mostly later; requires space and higher investment |

## 5. Upgrade Effects: How They Connect

The game should not make every upgrade an all-purpose bonus. Use these primary roles:

| System need | Main examples | Player-facing signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| More capacity | Sauna Extension, Better Changing Facilities | Fewer queue comments, more successful sessions |
| Better recovery / experience | Cold Plunge, Bathing Bridge, Water Station | Guests stay longer, stronger post-visit comments |
| More spend | Reception Shop, Towel Service | Higher guest spend, different purchase mix |
| More awareness / first impression | Sign and Program Frame, Lighting, outdoor deck | More interest and clearer concept signal |
| Stronger destination pull | Natural water, Bathing Bridge, Destination Outdoor Sauna | More non-local interest and marketing potential |

Appeal remains contextual. A roof plunge can be expensive but distinctive. A lake bridge can be naturally compelling but may not solve a poor program or weak service.

## 6. First Upgrade Decisions by Site

The starting choices should all make sense; the following are nudges, not answers:

| Site | Good early capacity response | Good early identity response | Good early spend response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Canal Workshop | Better Changing Facilities | Canal Bathing Bridge or Program Frame | Towel Service |
| Northside Depot | Sauna Extension | Outdoor Bench Terrace or Lighting | Reception Shop |
| Pine Lake Pavilion | Outdoor Relaxation Deck | Bathing Bridge or Program Frame | Towel Service |

This gives players a readable lesson: site context changes the value of the same decision, but it never declares a single mandatory build.

## 7. Asset Scope for the First Build

This is a content-facing asset outline, not the final production inventory.

### Reusable foundations

- one small set of environment tiles: grass, paving, water edge, road/forecourt, deck
- three location palettes: canal/brick, depot/urban, lake/forest
- three base facades sharing a compatible size and anchor system
- guest sprite set with a short walk cycle and a few static activity poses
- universal ambience: water, smoke, lights, simple traffic/bicycle movement

### Upgrade layers

- sign/program frame
- entry/service layer
- side extension layer
- terrace/deck layer
- cold-water layer
- bathing-bridge layer
- roof-facility layer
- lighting/planting cosmetic layer

### Explicitly out of the first asset build

- interior rooms and furniture
- unique bespoke construction animation per upgrade
- 40 complete building families
- a complete animation library for every Aufguss combination
