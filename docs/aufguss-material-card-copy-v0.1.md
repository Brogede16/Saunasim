# Sauna Sim Aufguss Material Card Copy v0.1

## Purpose

This is the first complete English player-facing copy library for the 30 approved Aufguss materials. Each card teaches sensory character, useful program role and a meaningful combination risk without medical claims, raw scores or an explicit recipe list.

Copy is informed by the material categories and supplier-backed sensory references in `aufguss-material-library-v0.1.md`. It describes the effect on the **Gus experience**, never a health effect on the guest.

## Everyday Materials

### Eucalyptus

**About**  
Crisp, cooling and clean. Eucalyptus puts a clear edge around steady heat and can make a recovery Gus feel freshly opened.

**Good to know**  
Use it as the Gus' one clear fresh note. Pairing it with peppermint, ice and several other sharp materials can make the session feel harsh rather than refreshing.

### Peppermint

**About**  
Bright and direct. Peppermint wakes up a short program or gives a warmer round a cool, deliberate turn.

**Good to know**  
Use it to add a cool contrast to orange, wood or a warm final round. Avoid combining it with eucalyptus and several other sharp herbs in the same Gus; the scent can become too intense.

### Lemon

**About**  
Light, clear and easy to place. Lemon brightens a social or daytime Gus without making the rest of the program too heavy.

**Good to know**  
Use it before wood or herb rounds when the program needs a fresh opening. If the rest of the Gus is built around coffee, tar or several dark woods, lemon needs a warmer middle round to avoid feeling disconnected.

### Orange

**About**  
Warm citrus with a softer finish. Orange is at home in welcoming evening sessions, especially when the heat is allowed to build gently.

**Good to know**  
Use it to soften sharper materials and make a social program more welcoming. Avoid placing it directly before a very hard heat finale unless the program deliberately moves from warm to intense.

## Craft Materials: Nordic Woods

### Nordic Birch

**About**  
Green, clean and recognisably Nordic. Birch is a reliable centre for a classic Gus and does not need a large show around it.

**Good to know**  
Use it with classic towelwork, folk or acoustic music, and a calm recovery finish. Avoid pairing it with very loud music or several dark wood scents; birch is a lighter, greener note.

### Spruce

**About**  
Brisk, resinous and forest-deep. Spruce gives a program an outdoor edge and a feeling of cooler air around the heat.

**Good to know**  
Use it before a cold-water or outdoor finish to connect the Gus to the setting. Avoid using it with mountain pine, Scots Pine and juniper in several consecutive rounds; guests will struggle to distinguish them.

### Scots Pine

**About**  
Dry wood, needles and a firm line through the heat. Scots Pine gives traditional towelwork a clear, grounded character.

**Good to know**  
Use it as the strong wood round in a traditional Gus. Add citrus, mint or a warmer spice elsewhere in a long program, or the whole sequence can become dry and repetitive.

### Fir

**About**  
Soft forest with a balsamic edge. Fir brings a forest note to a quiet Gus without the sharper character of spruce or pine.

**Good to know**  
Use it with Gentle Build, Ambient or a rest-deck finish. Add one clearer opening or final round in longer programs, or the Gus can become too flat.

## Craft Materials: Herbs

### Rosemary

**About**  
Dry, aromatic and alert. Rosemary brings definition to a daytime or rhythmic program and keeps herb-led rounds from drifting.

**Good to know**  
Use it to give a social or rhythmic Gus a clear herbal centre. Avoid adding it to a program that already uses peppermint, eucalyptus and fast electronic pacing; too many sharp elements make the session feel busy.

### Sage

**About**  
Soft, earthy and a little dry. Sage slows a busy program and gives it a quieter middle round.

**Good to know**  
Use it with quieter heat, wood notes, Green Tea or evening music. In a loud social Gus, pair it with a clear contrasting round or it will seem unrelated to the rest of the program.

### Thyme

**About**  
Warm, green and concentrated. Thyme makes a strong middle round when a program needs to settle into something more grounded.

**Good to know**  
Use it as a bridge between citrus and wood, or between a fresh opening and a ritual finish. Avoid combining it with cardamom, curry and other dense spice notes in the same middle round; the scent becomes crowded.

### Lavender

**About**  
Soft floral calm with a quiet finish. Lavender gives an evening program a clear recovery direction.

**Good to know**  
Use it with slower heat, Ambient or gentle acoustic music. Avoid following it with a sudden loud performance or hard finale unless the contrast is the stated point of the Gus.

## Premium Botanicals

### Juniper

**About**  
Dry, aromatic and distinctly Nordic. Juniper gives a ritual a more rugged edge and can make a destination Gus feel memorable.

**Good to know**  
Use it with water, outdoor recovery or a clear traditional theme. Give it its own round after citrus or florals; rushing straight into juniper can overpower the earlier scent.

### Mountain Pine

**About**  
Highland forest with a strong resinous presence. Mountain Pine supports a bold program that builds towards stronger heat.

**Good to know**  
Use it late in a Progressive Rounds or High-Heat Finale program. Do not use it as the first strong round; the later rounds will have little room to increase.

### Cedarwood

**About**  
Dry, warm wood with a slow premium character. Cedarwood gives an evening Gus depth without needing louder music or hotter heat.

**Good to know**  
Use it with quiet music, a warm evening setting and a rest finish. Do not place coffee, whisky and birch tar next to it in consecutive rounds; the program becomes too dark and heavy.

### Birch Tar

**About**  
Dark birch bark with an old smoke-sauna character. Birch Tar is a statement material for a clear traditional idea.

**Good to know**  
Use it in one carefully planned traditional round with a capable Aroma Master. Do not add it simply because it is premium; it can overpower every earlier material in the program.

## Distinctive Materials

### Hayflower

**About**  
Dry meadow, warm grass and a soft floral edge. Hayflower adds a light summer-meadow character without becoming sweet.

**Good to know**  
Use it with gentle heat, acoustic music or a relaxed social setting. Avoid placing it beside heavy resin, coffee or dark Event Essences; those stronger notes will hide it.

### Green Tea

**About**  
Clean leaf, a little bitterness and a composed finish. Green Tea supports a refined, quiet program with measured heat.

**Good to know**  
Use it with Gentle Build, Quiet Ritual or low-key music. Do not use it as the only subtle material in a dense show; louder scents and performance will make it disappear.

### Sea Buckthorn

**About**  
Tart, bright and northern. Sea Buckthorn adds a sharp coastal or daytime note to a Gus.

**Good to know**  
Use it to freshen warm woods and herbs, especially before water recovery. Avoid mixing it with several sweet or dark notes unless the program has a clear fresh-to-warm sequence.

### Cardamom

**About**  
Warm spice with a green aromatic lift. Cardamom gives an evening or social Gus a more unusual kind of welcome.

**Good to know**  
Use it with orange, cedarwood or folk music for a warm social Gus. Do not stack it with thyme, curry and other strong spices; the scent becomes too dense.

### Hinoki

**About**  
Dry cypress with a quiet, precise wood note. Hinoki supports a calm premium ritual without extra spectacle.

**Good to know**  
Use it with measured heat, Quiet Ritual and a patient Master. Avoid using it as the main scent in a loud, crowded event; guests may not notice it.

### Coffee

**About**  
Roasted, dark and recognisable. Coffee gives a morning or urban event Gus an immediate, unusual identity.

**Good to know**  
Use it with a clear morning, urban or event theme. Avoid adding it to a gentle recovery Gus; its roasted character can make the finish feel too heavy.

## Event Essences

### Beer & Hops

**About**  
Dry malt, bitter green hops and a social edge. Beer & Hops can make a folk, harbour or metal Gus feel like a real shared occasion.

**Good to know**  
Use it with folk, rock, metal or a harbour/industrial social theme. Do not add it to a polished quiet ritual without another clear reason; it can seem like a joke.

### Chilli Orange

**About**  
Sharp citrus with a heated edge. Chilli Orange gives an energetic program a final-round spark without needing another music change.

**Good to know**  
Use it in the final round of a controlled heat build. Do not use it early or with several other forceful materials; the Gus will feel intense from start to finish.

### Dark Chocolate & Mint

**About**  
Dark sweetness cut with cool mint. This is a high-contrast material for a playful late program or an intentionally theatrical turn.

**Good to know**  
Use it with disco, night sessions or a confident show Master. Do not combine it with other dessert-like or heavily spiced materials; the program becomes confusing rather than playful.

### Cappuccino

**About**  
Roasted coffee softened by a creamy impression. Cappuccino gives a morning event or city program an immediate, familiar hook.

**Good to know**  
Use it for a clearly named morning or city event. Without that context, guests may remember the coffee idea more than the heat or the craft.

### Whisky

**About**  
Warm oak, depth and a slower evening character. Whisky can give a rock, metal or cedar-led Gus a mature finish.

**Good to know**  
This is a fragrance profile, not a drink service. Use it after the program has already built warmth; do not pair it with several other dark notes, or the Gus becomes too heavy.

### Myrrh

**About**  
Dark resin with a balsamic, ceremonial pull. Myrrh gives a story-led Gus a feeling of focus and arrival.

**Good to know**  
Use it with a restrained heat build, deliberate performance and spacious music. Do not use it in a busy social session unless the program clearly signals a ceremonial turn; it can otherwise feel too solemn.

### Seaweed

**About**  
Saline, marine and deliberately unusual. Seaweed gives a coastal or floating Gus a clear water-based identity.

**Good to know**  
Use it with a coastal, floating or clearly maritime city concept. Pair it with a fresh or bold supporting round; without one, seaweed can feel unrelated to the rest of the Gus.

### Curry Spice

**About**  
Warm, complex and unmistakably polarising. Curry Spice is a special-event material, not a general-purpose herb.

**Good to know**  
Use it with a simple social or story-led event concept. Do not add it to an already crowded herb or Event Essence sequence; the program becomes noisy and hard to understand.

## Delivery Forms

### Water Pour

**About**  
The classic pour: direct, dependable and easy to read. Water Pour lets the material and heat plan do the work without adding another delivery effect.

**Good to know**  
Often the best choice when a Gus already has enough character. A program does not need ice, herbs or special equipment to feel complete.

### Scented Ice Round

**About**  
A slower release with a visible pause built into the round. Scented Ice gives a fresh material or quiet middle phase more time to develop.

**Good to know**  
Use it for one deliberate pause or a slower middle round. Adding it to every round makes a simple Gus longer and reduces how many sessions the room can run.

### Herbal Infusion

**About**  
A more tactile, plant-led way to bring a round into the sauna. Herbal Infusion makes woods and herbs feel like a prepared ritual rather than a standard pour.

**Good to know**  
Use it in a standard or long program with time for a deliberate middle round. Avoid loud music, fast transitions or too many materials; guests will not notice the extra care.

### Botanical Water

**About**  
A lighter plant-water infusion with a soft edge. Botanical Water carries natural materials gently and keeps a quieter Gus from becoming too dense.

**Good to know**  
Use it when the program needs a fresh natural round without adding much time. Do not rely on it alone for a bold finale; choose a stronger heat or performance element as well.

### Fresh Vihta

**About**  
A fresh branch ritual using birch, maple or juniper. Vihta adds a visible moment of traditional craft and a more natural, green presence around the heat.

**Good to know**  
Use it in a clearly named ritual and never assume guests want contact. Do not add it as decoration to an unrelated show; the tradition needs to be part of the program's actual idea.

### Rain Pour

**About**  
A fine, broad pour that changes the room more evenly than a single heavy ladle. Rain Pour gives a stronger heat phase shape and reach.

**Good to know**  
Use it with a Master who has a Rain Ladle and a program that is ready for a stronger room-wide heat phase. Do not use it in every round; it makes the program's heat changes harder to notice.

## Copy Review Rules

- Never state or imply a medical treatment, health benefit or breathing claim.
- Do not promise a tier, guest response or revenue outcome.
- Do not imply that a material is valid only at one location type.
- Keep the negative line about program coherence, pacing, expectation or sensory crowding, never about guest identity.
- Create 2-4 approved paraphrases for each card before implementation, while keeping this version as the factual tone reference.
