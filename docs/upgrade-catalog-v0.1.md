# Sauna Sim: Upgrade Catalogue v0.1

Last updated: August 23, 2026

## Purpose

This is the proposed first catalogue of 15 venue upgrades. Each upgrade has one primary gameplay job, a visible exterior result in the pixel world, and a clear relationship to player feedback. Interior operations are represented through the phone UI and the building's exterior transformation, never as a room cutaway.

The player begins by paying for an essential opening installation: a basic sauna unit, basic changing/service setup and entry handling. That opening work is required at every property type and is not counted among the 15 upgrades.

## Upgrade Rules

- An upgrade should be bought because it answers an observed opportunity or pressure, not because it is a universal bonus.
- Every functional upgrade must visibly change the venue or its surrounding activity.
- Cosmetic upgrades may exist separately, but do not occupy core progression slots.
- There are no pre-drawn empty upgrade slots. Prerequisites are logical, such as water access, roof structure, sufficient exterior space or an existing sauna.
- Site context changes cost and appearance, not whether a concept is "allowed".

## Visible Without an Interior Cutaway

The player must always see that an upgrade happened. The rule is not "no visual change"; it is "no top-down interior-room view." Every functional upgrade gets two forms of confirmation:

1. **Physical transformation:** A new exterior volume, deck, sign, access point, chimney, roof feature, service door, water facility or light treatment.
2. **Behavioural transformation:** Guests or venue ambience visibly behave differently after the improvement.

| Upgrade | Exterior change | What visibly changes afterwards |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entry Pavilion | New entrance canopy/annex | Shorter entrance queue and smoother guest flow into the building |
| Sauna Capacity Extension | Larger sauna wing, roofline or chimney | More guests enter around program times; stronger steam/heat activity |
| Towel Service | Service sign and towel point | Some arriving guests stop briefly at the entrance service point |
| Reception Shop | Window counter or street-facing hatch | Small purchase interaction at the facade; occasional guests leave with a bag/icon |
| Service Annex | Rear/side service volume and delivery detail | Occasional supply delivery and less visibly rushed entry activity |
| Outdoor Bench Terrace | Seating/deck layer | Guests sit, wait and talk outside rather than cluster at the door |
| Cold Plunge / Bathing Bridge | Tank, pool or water steps | Guests visibly use the facility after sauna sessions |
| Lighting / Program Frame | Lights, poster/emblem and signage | Stronger evening identity; program poster updates when the headline Aufguss changes |

This makes the venue feel alive and progressive without requiring us to animate inside rooms or produce interior asset sets.

## 1. First Improvements

### 1. Program Frame and Exterior Sign

**Primary job:** Awareness and clearer first impression.

**Visible result:** A program board, venue sign and warmer entrance treatment.

**Responds to:** "I did not know this place was here" and unclear concept feedback.

**Trade-off:** Increases attention before it improves the actual visit. It is strongest when the venue promise is real.

### 2. Water Station

**Primary job:** Basic comfort and recovery.

**Visible result:** A clearly visible water point at the entrance, terrace or deck.

**Responds to:** Guests who need a more considered recovery experience.

**Trade-off:** Low cost and low impact on revenue. It improves quality rather than creating a destination by itself.

### 3. Towel Service

**Primary job:** Convenience and optional guest spend.

**Visible result:** Towel basket/service sign at the entrance.

**Responds to:** Convenience visitors and guests who arrive without equipment.

**Trade-off:** Some guests bring their own towels. Do not expect universal uptake.

### 4. Entry Pavilion

**Primary job:** Reduce entry and exit bottlenecks.

**Visible result:** A more substantial entrance canopy, side annex or front-service volume.

**Responds to:** Queues, cramped arrival feedback and poor first/last impression.

**Trade-off:** The capacity benefit is measured through the phone, but the venue's arrival sequence must visibly look more capable.

### 5. Sauna Capacity Extension

**Primary job:** Increase session capacity.

**Visible result:** A slightly larger sauna volume, roofline or chimney expression.

**Responds to:** Sold-out sessions and sauna-seat queues.

**Trade-off:** More seats do not fix weak programs or poor service.

### 6. Evening Light Package

**Primary job:** Strengthen evening appeal and visible identity.

**Visible result:** Warm facade lamps, path lights and an evening glow layer.

**Responds to:** Weak first impression and a concept that needs a stronger mood.

**Trade-off:** It helps attention and atmosphere, but cannot make a bad visit good.

## 2. Venue Identity and Service

### 7. Outdoor Bench Terrace

**Primary job:** Better waiting comfort, social dwell time and exterior life.

**Visible result:** Benches and a small terrace/forecourt/deck.

**Responds to:** Guests who want somewhere to pause, wait or talk after a session.

**Trade-off:** Strongest where visible exterior space exists. It does not substitute for proper recovery facilities.

### 8. Reception Shop

**Primary job:** Optional spend and a more complete hosted experience.

**Visible result:** Window counter, shop sign or small service hatch.

**Responds to:** Guests looking for convenience, gifts or a more premium service moment.

**Trade-off:** Requires enough flow and staff attention to pay for itself.

### 9. Service Annex

**Primary job:** Improve service reliability and reduce staff strain.

**Visible result:** Small visible rear/side service annex, utility door and delivery detail.

**Responds to:** "The team seemed rushed" and repeated service-quality issues.

**Trade-off:** It is not flashy. Its value is operational consistency.

### 10. Outdoor Relaxation Deck

**Primary job:** Encourage longer stays and strengthen recovery/destination feel.

**Visible result:** A more substantial deck with loungers, planting and simple fire/heat detail where appropriate.

**Responds to:** Guests who want to cool down, stay longer or experience the setting.

**Trade-off:** Context matters. A roof deck, canal deck and forest deck all cost and look different.

### 11. Premium Program Equipment

**Primary job:** Make expensive high-end Aufguss programs easier to execute consistently.

**Visible result:** Program emblem/banner and small exterior cues; the main visual payoff occurs during Aufguss.

**Responds to:** Strong demand for elevated rituals or a player deliberately pursuing premium positioning.

**Trade-off:** Higher running costs. It is not an unlock gate: premium program content remains purchasable without it, just less efficient.

## 3. Major Venue Transformations

### 12. Cold Plunge Facility

**Primary job:** Create a strong recovery feature and new guest capacity point.

**Visible result:** Plunge tank, compact pool or an adapted roof plunge.

**Responds to:** Repeated recovery/cooling feedback and guests seeking a more complete sauna ritual.

**Trade-off:** Expensive in dense city locations and does not replace natural water's destination appeal.

### 13. Bathing Bridge

**Primary job:** Direct natural-water ritual and a major destination signature.

**Visible result:** Pier, steps, railings and water-entry activity.

**Responds to:** Water-oriented recovery, ritual and destination feedback.

**Trade-off:** Only feasible at appropriate waterfront sites. It creates attention but still needs safe capacity and good programs.

### 14. Sauna Extension

**Primary job:** Major session-capacity increase and stronger building silhouette.

**Visible result:** New side volume, extended roofline, larger chimney or attached sauna module.

**Responds to:** Persistent sold-out sessions, program demand and high queue pressure.

**Trade-off:** A construction project that carries cost and temporary operational pressure. More volume increases staffing and operating needs.

### 15. Signature Glass or Timber Wing

**Primary job:** High-prestige concept expression, extra capacity and stronger first impression.

**Visible result:** Glass wing in urban/waterfront settings or a timber/glass wing at nature sites.

**Responds to:** A venue that has outgrown its original identity and can support a more ambitious promise.

**Trade-off:** Large capital commitment. It can attract the wrong expectations if the rest of the venue cannot deliver a premium visit.

## Upgrade Coverage Check

| Feedback theme | Core responses |
| --- | --- |
| "I cannot find/understand this place" | 1, 6 |
| "The visit is cramped or queued" | 4, 5, 14 |
| "I need better recovery" | 2, 10, 12, 13 |
| "The service feels rushed" | 8, 9, staff hire |
| "The venue lacks a reason to stay" | 7, 10, 13, 15 |
| "The program needs more depth" | 11, better Master, program changes |
| "I will pay for a stronger experience" | 8, 11, 12, 15, price change |

## Suggested First Build Availability

Offer upgrades 1-7 early. Upgrades 8-11 appear once the first venue has operated long enough to generate meaningful feedback. Upgrades 12-15 are major investments available when their site and finances support them.

This is a content cadence, not an arbitrary research tree: most options can be seen early, but their cost and logical prerequisite determine whether they are sensible now.
