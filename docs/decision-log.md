# Sauna Sim Decision Log

Last updated: August 23, 2026

## Locked Design Decisions

- The game is a management sim about building a respected sauna brand through a distinct sauna concept, not a spreadsheet empire simulator.
- `Aufguss` is the primary term. Use `Aufguss Master`, `Show Aufguss`, `Traditional Aufguss`, and similar language consistently.
- New expansion opportunities should come from generated site offers rather than a world map where the player manually chooses any city.
- The player receives three generated, financially viable site offers when choosing a new location. Every offer has a visible physical starter base: a converted building, container compound, tent camp or floating sauna. It is not necessarily an existing sauna-ready property, but it is never an empty plot awaiting a free first structure.
- At least one of the three initial offers is an existing building. All offers must be priced so the player can afford the site and its essential opening work.
- Declining a site offer is free and does not remove the other current offers. Searching for a fresh batch is a deliberate broker search that uses game time; it is not a punishment for rejecting an offer.
- The player chooses a `Budget`, `Standard` or `Premium` broker search. The tier determines the financial range of sites sought and search duration. It does not buy random quality or guarantee success.
- A normal search should be short relative to the operating clock: as a starting balance target, Budget takes roughly one in-game hour, Standard two, and Premium three. The player may pay a tier-scaled broker commission to receive the next batch immediately; waiting normally costs no separate fee. Exact commissions and durations require playtesting.
- A completed broker search replaces all three current site offers as one new batch. The player chooses among three distinct directions rather than repeatedly retaining one preferred offer and filtering for a perfect match.
- Time is always running and the player does not pause the game. Future competition with other players requires a fair shared-time model.
- The target pacing is one in-game week per real 24-hour day, or roughly one in-game day every 3 hours and 25 minutes. This is a working balance target to validate in playtests.
- Weekly reviews therefore arrive once per real day. Live venue signals and rolling operational summaries must provide useful feedback before the review.
- Venues continue to operate automatically while the player is away and provide a concise return summary.
- Avoid mobile-game construction timers, paid skips and gameplay that blocks solely to make the player wait. If construction takes time, it must be a meaningful operational trade-off rather than a monetisation gate.
- Upgrade duration depends on a project's size and prestige and runs on real elapsed time, including while the player is away. Small changes complete quickly; larger builds receive a longer but still bounded real-time countdown.
- Players may pay additional in-game money for faster construction through an expensive rush or contractor option. This is an economic trade-off, not a paid skip.
- A venue continues to operate during major construction where plausible, with a relevant temporary operational downside.
- Construction completes automatically while the player is away; there is no manual claim step.
- Future competitive ranking should use shared weekly or monthly periods so every player is measured against the same calendar.
- Competitive ranking periods use real shared calendar time and stay separate from the compressed in-game operational calendar.
- Generated sites must not be objective traps. Every offer should support a viable strategy with different trade-offs, investment needs and concept fit.
- The player sees concrete site information such as size, rent or purchase price, outdoor/water potential and a local description, but not raw market-demographic values.
- Natural water is a valuable physical advantage for cold-water experiences and should normally make a location more expensive.
- The player can adapt a concept to a market or deliberately maintain a distinctive concept despite weaker immediate fit.
- A sufficiently distinctive, premium or exclusive venue can create new demand and attract guests who would not otherwise choose that location. Local fit shapes the starting opportunity; it does not permanently cap what a strong concept can achieve.
- Every site offer begins with an existing physical starter base: a converted building, container compound, tent camp or floating sauna. There are no empty plots and no player-built first structure.
- Container compounds and tent camps are modular existing venue families with strong visual identity but limited comfort and capacity at first.
- Container yards and tent sites cost less to open but begin with lower capacity and income. They still support meaningful early upgrades.
- A tent sauna remains on its original plot and grows only through tent-compatible modules; it does not transform into a conventional building.
- Installed sauna containers are fixed venue modules and cannot be moved or sold as a flexible inventory item.
- Every site begins with a visible, operational starter base. Existing buildings may require renovation and all venue families grow through later upgrades.
- The three starting forms should all remain commercially viable long term. Lower-cost tent and container venues can have lower physical throughput but compensate through low operating costs, a strong concept or a sought-after Aufguss Master; they must not be coded as permanent hard mode.
- Renting and buying are always available choices where plausible, but property ownership is usually a later-game financial decision.
- Higher rent should normally reflect stronger potential customer flow and city-location advantages, without guaranteeing success.
- Site surroundings should have some visible variation, but should not require a wholly unique art set for every location.
- Venue scenes use a layered composition system rather than bespoke art per site: a reusable building and upgrade layer sits on one of roughly 10-20 authored environment backdrops. Small foreground props, terrain edges and ambient effects vary the result further without multiplying the core asset workload.
- Each backdrop package supplies visible context and a small set of simulation tags affecting acquisition price, outdoor possibilities and guest appeal. It must not prescribe the entire concept or make a venue mechanically invalid.
- Reusable does not mean arbitrary: every backdrop and base building has a small setting tag set. The site generator only combines compatible pairs, such as warehouses with harbour, canal or rail/industrial backdrops, and cabins with forest, lake or coastal backdrops. This protects real-world plausibility without requiring every venue to be a unique asset.
- Each environment backdrop supports day, evening and night presentation through reusable palette, lighting and ambient-effect layers, not three separately drawn scenes. Window and exterior light, steam/smoke and a few context effects make operating hours visually legible.
- Natural-water access is always operational when a venue has a bathing bridge or equivalent access. In winter the presentation automatically shows a maintained opening in the ice; it is not gated behind a separate safety or technical upgrade.
- The guest animation scope includes entering and leaving natural water, with a winter presentation variant for an ice opening. This is a reusable water-interaction animation, not a bespoke animation per venue.
- Winter presentation follows a lightweight backdrop climate profile. Northern/nature-facing locations can show snow and ice; urban or mild-coastal locations may instead show darker, wet, cold conditions and more exterior lighting. Climate is visual context, not a separate geography-management system.
- Seasons apply only small, transparent demand modifiers to existing guest motivations and program components. Winter slightly favours recovery, ritual and cold-water experiences where available; summer slightly favours outdoor and social use. This must use the existing appeal model, add no new guest type or complex season-management layer, and never close a viable venue.
- Seasonal changes use fixed dates on the shared game calendar. This keeps seasonal demand, visuals and later multiplayer ranking conditions identical for every player.
- The initial functional module set is reception, changing, sauna, shower/bath, cold water and lounge. The visual system should make these from a small reusable asset set.
- Guests should visibly use the venue where practical: walking, sitting on benches and entering water. Visual activity must stay within a manageable, documented asset and animation scope.
- The simulation may use individual guest visits because venues normally serve roughly 30-100 guests. The pixel scene must reflect demand and crowding without needing a literal visual representation of every guest at all times.
- A venue may run several Aufguss programs per day, constrained by opening hours and capacity. The player should not initially need to place each program manually on a detailed timeline. Aufguss is an early, high-impact way to shape venue identity.
- The player sets each venue's opening time, closing time and days of operation. The game does not reduce this to a fixed opening-hours preset.
- Longer opening hours increase potential demand and Aufguss capacity, but also increase wages, workload and operating cost.
- Guest availability changes by time of day and weekday/weekend. Model this through visit motivation, local rhythm and work/life availability rather than rigid age stereotypes.
- Weekdays and weekends should have different demand patterns without requiring detailed day-by-day staff scheduling.
- Guests pay entry and may make optional purchases in shops, cafes, or amenities. Not every guest uses every service; some bring their own equipment.
- Every venue has its own management menus. The player can inspect and change that venue's programs, staff, facilities, shop offer, finances and guest feedback while observing its exterior scene.
- A venue shop uses a small, readable product assortment. The player selects what is offered and sees sales performance; detailed manual stock ordering is out of scope for the first design track.
- A shop becomes available only when the venue has a Reception Shop or visible service hatch.
- The initial shop categories are drinks, towels/bathrobes, small wellness products and snacks.
- The player chooses a small assortment of roughly 3-5 products. The system replenishes stock automatically.
- Shop products use fixed or system-managed prices; the player does not individually price every product.
- Every shop sale has an automatic underlying procurement cost, so the shop contributes a margin rather than pure revenue. Restocking is automatic but visible in the venue's financial reporting.
- Guest visit motivations influence optional purchases. Social guests may favour drinks/snacks, while premium-oriented guests may favour bathrobes or wellness products.
- Stronger venue/chain identity can unlock merchandise tied to the venue name or a signature Aufguss program.
- Branded merchandise has a higher procurement cost, including a small-batch/order premium, but supports a stronger branded offer and a higher system-managed sale value.
- Every new venue begins with a suggested visible entry price. The player sets the final entry price before opening and may later change it only between operating periods.
- Guest booking mechanics are out of scope for the first design track. Aufguss increases guest interest and attendance without asking the player to manage reservations.
- Standard Aufguss is included with entry. The player may price selected premium or show Aufguss sessions as paid extras.
- A higher entry price reduces some first-time demand, but can succeed when the concept and delivered experience support premium value.
- Expansion opportunities appear independently of venue stability, but cannot be bought without sufficient cash or additional borrowing capacity.
- A separate Chain Overview presents each owned venue as a small visual building card/mini-scene rather than a spreadsheet table. It is the portfolio and expansion screen, with chain finances, Operations Managers, current site offers and venue-sale actions.
- Ranking is deliberately separate from Chain Overview, in its own dedicated screen.
- Ranking is visible from the first venue as the player's current rank among simulated reference chains.
- `Game Dev Tycoon` is a structural reference: a readable, animated pixel world should make management outcomes tangible without requiring a complex simulation presentation.
- The simulation should hide most raw market numbers. The engine can know the truth; the player should mostly infer what a local audience wants through outcomes, guest feedback, and performance.
- Rankings matter as a separate long-term standing signal, but do not alter local demand or create active local competitors.
- Guest taste should not be framed as `culture`. Preferred working term: `Guest Preferences` or `Market Preferences`.
- The first guest model uses five primary visit motivations: rest and recovery, ritual and tradition, social experience, convenient routine, and special premium experience.
- Every location has a hidden mix of these motivations that the player learns through operating results and reviews.
- Some guests choose venues for proximity and convenience; others travel for a distinctive program, destination quality or strong brand.
- Individual guests may return over time, creating recognisable local regulars rather than only anonymous one-off visits.
- The player receives qualitative retention signals such as "more guests return" or "many try the venue once" instead of raw hidden percentages.
- Every player-facing guest comment must identify a concrete, actionable topic: price/value, Aufguss, queue/capacity, staff/service, recovery facility, awareness or atmosphere.
- Daily feedback stays light. Weekly reviews provide 1-3 useful observations; monthly reviews reveal durable retention, demand and reputation patterns.
- Retention is more important to venue reputation than raw one-off visit count.
- A premium price can be good value when the delivered experience supports it.
- A guest may praise one part of the visit and criticise another. Feedback must map criticism to an available improvement through venue, program, price, staff, capacity or marketing.
- The player should be able to sell a sauna. Valuation should react to earnings, investments, wear, and brand or reputation, and brand value can be positive or negative.
- A venue may be sold while it has debt. Sale value includes the site, building and installed upgrades, then deducts outstanding debt alongside the other valuation factors.
- Weather is cosmetic in the first design track.
- Taxes are out of scope for the first design track.
- Financial investing outside the sauna business, such as stocks or bonds, is out of scope.
- Loan repayment should stay simple and mostly automatic.
- The player starts with enough capital to choose one of the initial site offers without being forced into a loan immediately.
- Loans are a simple, visible borrowing choice with automatic periodic repayment; do not model interest-rate negotiation or loan terms in detail.
- Rent, wages and operating costs are automatic. The player receives readable weekly and monthly financial reporting.
- Prices are shown as concrete currency amounts, not only abstract budget/standard/premium bands.
- Bankruptcy is possible, but only after the player has exhausted available borrowing options or deliberately declares bankruptcy.
- A very large end-state is roughly dozens of saunas; around 50 locations is an extreme late-game scale, not an early target.
- A venue may be acquired as an existing building or created on a leased/purchased plot. There is no artificial hard cap on the number of sites or saunas; expansion is constrained by capital, debt capacity, available opportunities, market fit and management complexity.
- Saunas can vary in size.
- Building customization should stay light and readable. The player should make simple style and expansion choices rather than draw walls tile by tile.
- The primary visual direction is a readable top-down pixel-art bathhouse with classic 16-bit handheld-RPG clarity. Venues should feel like small, living places built from rooms, props, guests, and expansion modules; they should not be represented only by management dashboards.
- The primary venue view is exterior-only. The player sees a building, its site, outdoor facilities and guests entering or using relevant exterior features; interior rooms are managed through UI rather than shown as cutaways.
- Indoor capacity and guest experience should be communicated through light status markers at the building, not by simulating visible interior paths.
- Buildings are primarily many facade and location variants, not many entirely distinct mechanical building types.
- Upgrades must not be telegraphed as fixed empty construction slots. Their visual construction and transformation should be a discovery and reward, in the spirit of satisfying upgrade-game reveals.
- Natural water and outdoor facilities are the most visually distinctive upgrades. Shops, towel service and improved changing facilities are mainly indoor upgrades.
- Cosmetic choices such as facade colour, signage, plants and lighting may exist, but meaningful exterior additions such as pools and bathing bridges must have gameplay effects.
- Major upgrades should visibly improve a building's silhouette. The same base building may develop in substantially different directions through upgrades and surroundings.
- Every venue uses one fixed exterior camera and scene footprint throughout its life. The initial composition reserves enough buildable space for plausible late upgrades; growth fills, extends into or activates that existing scene rather than switching to a larger map or camera view. This protects place identity and keeps the art pipeline reusable.
- Upgrade placement is fixed per scene and upgrade type. The player chooses what to build, not an arbitrary left/right coordinate; this preserves authored exterior composition and avoids mirrored/collision-prone asset variants.
- Facade materials and colours are fixed by the building type and its upgrade path. Do not add a player-facing colour/material customisation system; venue identity comes from site, silhouette, visible facilities, signage, programs and activity.
- The player names each venue. Its name appears automatically on appropriate exterior signage, in that venue's menus and on eligible branded merchandise; no separate logo editor is required for the first version.
- All player-facing game copy, including guest names, profile cards, feedback, menus and program labels, is English. Danish remains the working design language only.
- Every scene template contains a small authored guest-route graph, not free-form pathfinding. Shared route nodes include street/arrival, entrance, exit, terrace, service hatch and relevant water access. Building and upgrade modules expose fixed compatible route anchors.
- The simulation selects a visible guest action from actual venue use; the renderer plays the associated short route and animation. A guest can walk to the entrance, disappear into the interior, later reappear, sit outside, use the shop, enter water, bob/swim briefly and return. Inactive facilities do not receive routes or guest traffic.
- Guest animation remains a shared library: four-direction walk, door enter/exit, sit/stand, shop stop, water enter/exit and water idle, with a winter ice-opening variant. Scenes reuse these along authored paths rather than needing bespoke movement animation per site.
- Built pools support a short, contained swim loop as well as entering, exiting and idle-in-water states. This is a shared pool interaction animation, not free swimming/pathfinding across the scene.
- Pixel characters and animations are produced as an original shared sprite system: approve one body scale, palette, outline treatment and anchor point first, then derive all actions from that master. Reference images may guide mood, clothing and proportions but should not be copied as third-party sprite art.
- The approved guest character style uses a shared master body and animation set, not a bespoke sheet per individual. Runtime palette variants provide hair colour, skin tone, swimwear and towel variation without creating new frames. The initial release includes a small set of distinct body and hairstyle silhouettes from the start; each silhouette has its own shared action sheets where necessary, rather than being deferred or created per individual.
- **Guest visual variation scope (locked 2026-08-25):** the "small set" above is concretely at least two genders, 3 body types and 10 hairstyles, all sharing the same action-sheet rig. Skin tone and hair colour use realistic, varied palette ramps (not stylised/limited fantasy tones). A guest may also carry an eye-colour variant and one optional small accessory layer: sunglasses, a piercing or a sauna hat. All of these are runtime palette/layer swaps on the one shared master body, per the palette-variation rule below - none of them are bespoke per-guest frames. This multiplies the production layer count (body × hair × skin × eyes × optional accessory) well beyond the original two-or-three-silhouette assumption, so treat the layer/anchor pipeline as the hard part, not the frame count of any one action.
- **Canal guest need mix and age profile (locked 2026-08-26):** Canal's five canonical guest needs are weighted, never a hard rule - Routine 25%, Social 25%, Recovery 20%, Ritual 20%, Special premium 10% (implemented in `src/sim/guestWeek.ts`'s `CANAL_NEED_WEIGHTS`, matched to the tagged Repair Workshop routes in `venue-fit-archetypes-v0.1.md`). Guest age uses a soft mixture, not a clamp: city venues like Canal skew younger (peak 20-35, `CANAL_AGE_PROFILE`), while a countryside location would use an older peak instead - either way a real share of every batch is still drawn from the full adult range, so an atypical guest for that location is always possible, just proportionally rarer. Money/price tolerance is intended to skew the same direction as age typically implies (city = more price headroom) but is not yet separately implemented per location, since only Canal exists. "A guest or several may well like something the others don't at all, it just has to be proportionally fewer visits" (owner) is the general rule behind every weighted system here, not only this one.
- **Guest age range widened to 18-100 (locked 2026-08-26)**, replacing the earlier 19-74 prototype range; `CANAL_AGE_PROFILE`'s 20-35 city peak is unchanged.
- **Small, balanced elder pace/spend trade-off (locked 2026-08-26):** a very old guest is up to ~6% slower to move through the venue, balanced by up to ~5% more spend/dwell time - both effects are smooth curves that stay at zero until well past retirement age, deliberately unnoticeable at typical ages and balanced against each other rather than a one-sided penalty. This is an operational-pace effect, not a taste preference, so it does not conflict with `guest-behaviour-model-v0.1.md`'s "age never drives which sauna style someone likes" rule. Implemented as `elderPaceProfile(age)` in `src/sim/guestWeek.ts`, tested, but deliberately not yet wired into `canalBalance.ts`'s protected ledger or the save schema - Canal's own guest mix skews young, so there is nothing to verify it against yet; it is ready for whichever location's simulation needs it first.
- **Recovery-capacity pressure is never auto-restricted, only made visible before the player commits (locked 2026-08-26).** A player can still schedule a Gus that overloads shower/cold-plunge capacity - the game never blocks it - but the real, felt consequence (`coldRecoveryQueueLoss()` in `canalBalance.ts`: lost visits, a named bottleneck, frustrated guest feedback) is now also previewed factually in the programme builder before the player runs the week, not only shown after, in the same objective tone as the loan menu ("here are the numbers", never "this is a bad idea"). This extends the existing "player evaluates, nothing is auto-restricted" rule (see the loan-menu decision above) to programme scheduling.
- **A scheduled Gus is pre-booked/expected attendance; only shared recovery facilities queue (locked 2026-08-26).** See `aufguss-system-v0.1.md`'s dedicated note - this is deliberate, matching how a real timed Aufguss works, not a missing queue mechanic.
- **Guest hair/age correlation (locked 2026-08-26):** older guests are more likely to be bald or grey/white-haired, as a smooth, continuous probability curve, not an age cutoff - implemented in `src/content/guestAppearance.ts`'s `agingShare()`. A young guest can still be bald or grey-haired and most old guests are still neither; this is flavour only and never feeds back into gameplay behaviour, matching `guest-behaviour-model-v0.1.md`'s existing "age never drives taste" rule.
- **Third body/gender option: non-binary, flat-chested, with a small top-surgery-style chest scar (locked 2026-08-26).** This is representation, not a sexualised detail: the scar is a small, restrained pixel-art mark at the same treatment level as any other body detail (per `pixel-art-production-guide-v0.1.md`'s "restrained texture" rule), never a focal point, close-up or bare-chest pose. It sits in the same slot as the other two genders, so the base-body count becomes 3 genders x 3 body types = 9 shared bodies, not a special-cased tenth body or a separate system. No guest-facing UI ever labels or calls out a guest's gender or body type; it is purely a visual variation axis like hair or skin tone, never a stat, dialogue topic or filter.
- Palette variation is an explicit rendering feature: source art reserves discrete shade groups for hair, skin, swimwear and towels, and the renderer maps each group to the guest's selected palette. A text/data value selects a palette, but the code and asset convention perform the actual pixel replacement.
- Staff and especially Aufguss Masters may use authored visual identities and extra performance frames, while ordinary guests remain generated combinations of the shared sprite system.
- Building and upgrade appeal is a core contextual system: its effect depends on the location and the guests who otherwise visit there. Keep the model understandable; do not turn it into a broad collection of isolated penalties.
- Do not use blunt aesthetic rules such as "glass belongs in the city". Most site effects should be subtle, with only obvious mismatches carrying a strong consequence.
- Treat site-specific construction as feasibility and cost, not arbitrary appeal punishment. For example, a city cold-water pool may become a more expensive rooftop facility rather than an impossible or inherently poor choice.
- An upgrade may appeal to one guest preference while being less appealing to another, but the detailed formula remains hidden behind clear qualitative feedback.
- The exterior and outdoor area create a first impression. The real experience, particularly Aufguss and service, determines post-visit reviews and reputation.
- Marketing should communicate a venue's promise before a visit and help attract the right guests; it must not override a poor in-venue experience.
- Marketing is represented by simple concrete choices such as signage, program posters, local campaigns and opening events, not by an abstract slider.
- Marketing is optional early on: reputation and word of mouth can grow a good venue without it.
- Specific Aufguss programs may be marketed by their own name, scent and mood.
- Do not create a separate bait-and-switch mechanic in which effective marketing deliberately produces poor reviews. Marketing should promote real experiences; capacity and execution can still affect the outcome.
- As brand awareness grows, marketing should become cheaper or more effective.
- Do not add exterior deterioration states merely to visualise wear.
- Avoid bespoke construction animation for every upgrade. Use a small reusable construction-and-reveal language: the upgrade's own field becomes a compact active work area with restrained steam, temporary screening/material detail and a visible real-time countdown when tapped. The completed upgrade supplies the main visual reward.
- Use small ambient pixel animation to make venues live: where appropriate, chimney smoke, water movement, light city traffic, guests and other environmental activity.
- Pixel art should favour simple reusable tiles, broad colour areas and clear silhouettes over illustration-like texture. Most props are static; animation is limited to short guest walk cycles, water, steam, fire and a few deliberate Aufguss actions.
- Individual visible guests are tappable/clickable. Their compact profile card shows a generated name, age, main visit motivation, current activity, what they currently like and dislike, and a short qualitative visit reaction when enough of the visit has happened. It must explain experience rather than expose hidden formulas or reduce guests to stereotypes.
- Aufguss is the exception to the restrained-animation rule: distinct programs should have a recognisable visual performance language through multi-frame towel work, heat/damp patterns, scent colour accents and sound cues. Existing scene lighting may provide ambient context, but lighting is not a player-configured Aufguss component or a per-program asset requirement.
- An Aufguss program is composed from clear choices: heat, duration, scent, music or mood, and performance style. The player may name programs.
- A program may optionally add a cold-water phase and/or a recovery finish. The depth of this ritual is the player's choice, subject to physical venue capability.
- Special ingredients, scents and performance styles should normally be available rather than held behind a deep unlock tree; premium options are chiefly limited by price.
- Aufguss capacity is determined by the number of sauna rooms, their capacity and available staff, not by a fixed global cap on active programs. Larger venues may run multiple programs simultaneously in different rooms.
- Upgrades may unlock Aufguss components when there is a clear physical link. This is a capability unlock, not an arbitrary research tree: for example, a cold plunge or bathing bridge enables a cooling/recovery phase, and an extra sauna room enables parallel program tracks.
- The game uses an internal, hidden appeal vector that maps each program's components, price, facilities and Master fit to the five guest visit motivations.
- Program identity should use reusable visual language: a distinct emblem or poster, scent/steam colour accents, music direction and shared towel-work patterns. Do not require a bespoke animation set for every possible combination.
- Staff should generally be able to run available programs. Specialists improve quality and have recognisable strengths with certain program styles rather than acting as hard access gates.
- Masters are linked to eligible program styles/programs rather than permanently assigned to rooms; automatic scheduling chooses available compatible Masters.
- The player chooses programs and a session count per sauna room. The game fits them into opening hours and capacity rather than requiring detailed calendar placement.
- Standard programs are included with entry; premium programs may charge an exact extra amount per guest and always incur a visible running cost.
- Programs can be excellent for some guest preferences and merely adequate for others; they are not universally right or wrong.
- A failed Aufguss should mainly affect that session and its local reviews, not impose an outsized permanent brand penalty.
- Staff identity should come from traits, style, and performance personality rather than appearance-based optimization.
- The player starts alone as the owner and hires staff as the venue grows.
- The owner is an unseen operational role, not a customisable or visible avatar. The player manages the venue through a flexible management interface while observing the exterior pixel world; it does not need to visually resemble a phone.
- The game is designed mobile-first in portrait orientation. This does not imply mobile-game wait gates, paid skips or a phone-themed fiction; it means touch-readable controls, a compact persistent HUD and management panels that preserve the exterior scene.
- Starting alone means the owner can cover basic operations, but not deliver consistently strong Aufguss, service or capacity at scale.
- Each staff member should use only 2-3 clear attributes, including a recognisable style; avoid a large stat sheet.
- All staff roles can be available from the start. Their cost and fit, rather than arbitrary role unlocks, determine when they make sense to hire.
- The base staff catalogue includes Reception Host, Aufguss Master, Service Host, Shop/Cafe Host, technical support and a later Venue Manager role.
- In small venues, a Reception Host can cover both entry/reception and shop service.
- The Service Host primarily protects guest flow, comfort and venue service quality.
- Technicians are hireable chain staff. They travel between venues to install, maintain and repair eligible technical facilities; their hire/wage cost and each repair's material/travel cost are visible. One technician cannot repair several locations at once.
- Venue Managers are not required to own multiple venues. Their precise value must be concrete and cannot be a generic percentage bonus; the intended role is to reduce multi-site complexity by handling approved low-risk local operations under a player-selected strategy.
- A later chain-level Operations Manager is hired as staff for the whole chain, not assigned per venue. This role creates scale advantages across procurement, operations, marketing, construction and staff development.
- Operations Managers should have a small set of broad traits rather than a single fixed specialisation. Their traits influence procurement, operating efficiency and marketing effectiveness to varying degrees.
- Operations Managers can attend expensive courses over time to improve these capabilities. Courses are a meaningful investment, not a free unlock track.
- Staff receive ongoing wages. A skilled Aufguss Master should be financially worthwhile when assigned well, and can run recurring programs as part of normal staffing rather than being an occasional novelty.
- Staff may improve through experience, without becoming a deep RPG progression system.
- New applicants with different personalities, styles and specialities should appear over time.
- Workload should influence service and Aufguss quality through a simple model, not a complex burnout simulation.
- Staff work automatically from the venue's programs, opening hours and facilities; the player does not micromanage shifts.
- Staff should only leave because they are dismissed or unpaid. Avoid voluntary turnover and HR-drama systems.
- A staff member's style affects which programs they execute especially well, but does not make other programs impossible.
- Wellness add-ons and support amenities are part of progression.
- A chain does not need every location to be radically different. The player should be allowed to standardize a concept across multiple sites if that is strategically strong.
- The canal is a location-layer reference, not a locked venue named "Canal Workshop". A venue is generated as a compatible location + plot + building base + economy variation. Location-specific and building-specific upgrades remain separate but combine in the completed exterior scene.
- The locked first location families are Canal, Harbour, Industrial District, Forest Lake, Coast, Beach, Rural Plot, Water Plot, Urban Lot and Hotel Rooftop. Dense Urban District remains removed: Urban Lot and Hotel Rooftop restore city appeal while containing all construction inside a visually controlled player-owned footprint.
- Urban Lot always begins with an existing Tent Camp or Container Compound. Hotel Rooftop always begins with an existing Rooftop Sauna beside the lift/stair core. Neither uses empty plots or free first-structure construction.
- Self-built timber sauna was removed. The game has no free-placement or first-structure construction system.
- A chosen building base remains that building type for the life of the venue. It may receive only its own compatible modules; a depot cannot upgrade into a warehouse. Larger building types enter the game as separate purchasable/rentable site offers.
- Tent and container bases are the explicit modular exception: they may add matching tent or container units as visible expansions, but remain that base family and keep its realistic capacity envelope.
- Rural Plot is broadened into a rural-property family: it may generate an existing tent camp, container compound or country estate with its existing barn/driftsbygning. The house and barn are one proposed permanent building compound, with one coordinated future upgrade and art requirement.
- Harbour service building was considered and rejected as a building base. It is not part of the building library or future art scope.
- Every building base has a realistic operational capacity envelope. Capacity is represented through sauna capacity, arrival/changing flow, service capacity and outdoor recovery capacity. Visible upgrades improve specific bottlenecks but cannot turn a tent or compact house into an implausibly large venue.
- Saunatents and container bases are compatible with industrial plots as modest temporary/modular starts. They retain their small realistic operational envelope and lack natural-water advantages there.
- A former kursted/badesanatorium is a candidate large premium building base for scenic coast or rural settings. It is represented as a sauna/wellness venue, not a separate hotel simulation.
- Canal has a locked set of ten compact visible upgrades: improved entrance, reception/shop window, changing/service wing, extra sauna volume, canal glass front, canal terrace, canal shower/cooling point, bathing bridge, floating sauna platform, and roof plunge/roof terrace. They are player choices rather than a mandatory sequence.
- Harbour has a locked ten-upgrade set for compatible industrial waterfront buildings: restored loading doors, quay shop, larger sauna hall, service volume, glass front, quay terrace, outdoor Aufguss yard, eligible harbour steps, floating sauna raft and roof deck/plunge.
- Outdoor steam effects and outdoor showers are shared, reusable animation/facility assets. Their materials and placement adapt to the compatible location rather than requiring a unique asset design for every venue.
- Upgrade ownership is locked: locations own only terrain/water/shore/outdoor-access modules; buildings own facade, shop, service, sauna-volume, roof and attached-wing modules. A venue UI combines compatible choices, but the data and art systems keep these sets separate.
- Forest Lake has a locked ten-module location set: arrival path/lighting, forest terrace, lake deck, bathing bridge, lake shower, floating rest platform, cooling path, forest clearing, covered lakeside shelter and outdoor lakeside program space.
- Any functional visible facility must include its authored guest-route and interaction anchors before it is approved. Water routes include approach, optional shower, entry, water activity, exit and return; decorative-only bridges and facilities are not allowed.
- Narrow townhouse, former laundry, cinema/theatre foyer, small civic building and small former public-bath building bases were removed with Dense Urban District. They are not part of the future art, upgrade or asset scope.
- Beach service building was replaced by Floating Sauna as a proposed building base. Floating Sauna is only generated on Water Plot, a predominantly water scene with a very small shore/strand landing and fixed pier/bridge. It is the sole permitted building base there; Canal, Harbour and ordinary Beach retain their own land-building systems.
- Water Plot may add a late Floating Recovery Pontoon, and Floating Sauna may later add a second connected small sauna pod as a capacity expansion.
- Slatted timber wind/privacy screens are shared compatible exterior modules for Coast, Beach, Hotel Rooftop and Urban Lot.
- Outdoor changing tents/pods are shared water-route facilities with guest entry/exit animation and location-adapted art.
- A mobile/sailing sauna is approved only as a later post-v0.1 special type; it is excluded from the first production and asset scope.
- Warm outdoor spa is a locked shared facility with four visual variants: raised wild bath, sunken spa pool, rooftop spa and floating spa pool. It has shared step-in, seated, exit, steam, bubble and splash animation support; compatibility is constrained by venue scale and physical setting.
- Every purchasable asset must receive a data card covering primary effect, appeal profile, revenue mechanism, one-off/recurring costs, capacity/routes and visual-animation package. Generic operations/storage/staff-efficiency bonuses are prohibited until separately designed.
- The first visual and code vertical slice will validate one compatible canal combination. It starts immediately after basic conversion; no terrace, exterior shop or bathing bridge is assumed until the selected building and upgrade package are defined.
- Facilities are never withheld behind arbitrary progression when the player has cash or responsible borrowing capacity. Prices, running costs, asset-backed borrowing and ability to service repayments create the trade-off instead.
- Loans remain available even when the current cash flow is weak or an upgrade only makes financial sense after completion. The loan menu shows only objective terms such as borrowed amount, scheduled repayment and total debt; it does not predict a successful outcome or issue a judgemental risk warning. The player evaluates the project; automatic repayments and the eventual bankruptcy rules remain real.
- Loans are general chain capital, not construction-financed projects. The player may use borrowed cash for upgrades, operations or expansion, and automatic repayment begins on the normal loan schedule immediately after borrowing, regardless of how the cash is spent.
- The player sees one combined borrowing limit for the whole chain, rather than separate loans per venue. Behind it, the game calculates the limit from the combined value of buildings and upgrades, actual earnings over time and existing debt. A strong sauna can therefore help finance a new one, without the player administering separate loans per place.
- All live and offline progress uses one deterministic real-time simulation contract. Construction, repair and staff-travel completion, venue schedules, feasible Aufguss, guest operating blocks, costs, debt and reports resolve in the same chronological order whether the app is open or closed.
- Every purchasable field, room and upgrade requires one complete data card: compatibility, authored field, channel-specific capacity, routes, economy, experience effect, condition rule, build state and visual package. Assets without this contract cannot enter production.
- Local delivered experience remains the dominant source of local demand. Brand Value has capped, diminishing effects only; rank gives no direct economic advantage and simulated/reference chains never alter the player's local market.
- Negative cash opens factual player choices. Automatic bankruptcy happens only at a financial settlement where due obligations cannot be paid and no realistic approved loan or venue-sale option remains.
- **Negative-cash grace rule (resolves `system-coherence-audit-v0.3.md` item #5, 2026-08-25):** the grace period is not a fixed number of weeks; it lasts exactly as long as `borrowingRoom` (combined credit capacity minus outstanding debt) stays above zero. While it does, the player may freely "Continue at risk" every settlement, take a loan, or declare bankruptcy voluntarily. The moment `borrowingRoom` reaches zero while cash is still negative, "Continue at risk" is withdrawn: the player must take whichever loan still fits, or declare bankruptcy. This ties the boundary to the player's actual remaining financial options rather than an arbitrary week count, and keeps bankruptcy a deliberate choice, never a surprise. Implemented in `gameStore.continueAtRisk()` / `gameStore.declareBankruptcy()` in `src/sim/game.ts`.
- Aufguss Masters use three visible `0-10` craft ratings: Heat Craft, Aroma Craft and Performance Craft. Paid courses improve one rating at a time with escalating late-level cost; workload remains a derived scheduling consequence rather than a fourth stat.
- Masters have basic equipment and may receive individual towel sets, hand fans and infusion kits. Equipment improves one bounded delivery component, never total income or an unrestricted final-satisfaction multiplier.
- Outdoor Aufguss visibly shows the scheduled Master walking to the field, preparing and performing with the actual equipped tool category. The sprite pipeline uses shared body, pose and transparent tool layers.
- Every Aufguss program is evaluated through five separate internal `0-100` dimensions: Craft Coherence, Delivery Readiness, Guest Fit, Value Fit and Novelty. The editor exposes only Craft Coherence and Delivery Readiness, because these are directly actionable before scheduling; the other dimensions are learned through feedback and results. The game must not merge them into one universal recipe score.
- Music is a first-class Aufguss choice, represented by reusable genre profiles rather than licensed individual tracks. It must support the program's heat, performance and promise, but deliberate contrasts such as a well-framed Metal Gus can be highly successful. Masters may have one or two non-blocking music-affinity tags; guest response uses individual preferences and visit motivations, never demographic stereotypes.
- Every Aufguss may select one highlighted recovery finish: No Added Finish, Cold Plunge, Natural Water Dip, Outdoor Shower, Rest Deck or Refreshment Finish. A finish requires its visible compatible facility and authored guest route, but the underlying program always remains runnable without it.
- Aufguss timing is derived from the chosen base format plus its actual rounds, delivery forms and performance transitions. Recovery finishes extend the guest journey and recovery-facility demand after the sauna room is free; they do not falsely occupy the sauna room. The editor shows rounded room time, total guest-journey time and the resulting frequency/capacity consequence.
- The selected Aufguss intent is the primary input to the editor's one optional English program-name suggestion. The suggested title also reflects actual selected materials, music, delivery, setting and recovery where relevant; it is never a forced name or a generic fantasy-title list.
- An Aufguss has one, two or three ordered aroma rounds. Every round is exactly one material plus one delivery form. Extra rounds add real room time, per-session input cost and Aroma Craft demand; they are never an automatic quality bonus.
- `Scented Ice Round` is one single delivery form. Do not create crushed-ice, ice-sphere, snow-sphere or seasonal player-facing variants; the shared ice/bucket animation and one rule set are sufficient.
- No location or building family may depend on a particular material family to make strong Aufguss. Materials are available across all compatible venues; place affects interpretation, marketing and guest fit rather than inherent program viability. The later balance pass must protect viable low-cost, craft-led and capacity-appropriate premium routes for every venue.
- The first Aufguss material library contains 30 items: 4 Everyday Materials, 8 Craft woods/herbs, 4 Premium Botanicals, 6 Distinctive Materials and 8 Event Essences. Event Essences are deliberate high-cost special-program materials, not generic quality upgrades; at most one may appear in a program.
- Saved Aufguss programs begin Unproven and reveal their visible composition tier after one completed session: Bad (red), Normal (grey), Rare (blue), Iconic (gold) or one of six fixed Ultimate combinations (platinum-white with restrained warm shimmer). The tier is calculated only from the Gus combination, never Master, venue, price or session history. Those operating factors separately determine whether the program is delivered well and considered good value. A core composition change creates an Unproven revised version, which must be run once to reveal its tier. Ultimate is a discoverable composition mark, not a sixth star or automatic income source.
- Every first completed Unproven Gus shows a compact review card: the revealed composition tier, actual first-session result, one human guest/Steam Guide observation and, if relevant, one actionable warning. Later sessions use ordinary feedback and reports rather than repeating the discovery card.
- Every completed Gus also receives a separate Execution Tier: Bad, Normal, Rare or Iconic, based on its actual Master, craft/style/music fit, equipment, workload, scheduling, price and guest response. The active venue receives a separate Venue Fit tier based on location + building base + compatible visible upgrades, including capacity, recovery and local guest rhythm. The overview turns Composition Tier + latest Execution Tier + current Venue Fit into a 1-5 whole-star rating that is limited by the weakest tier and gains a bonus only when all three are Rare or stronger. Tapping it always reveals all three tiers and the actionable weak point.
- Venue Fit has direct but capped program-specific benefits: targeted attendance, price acceptance, post-session retention and word of mouth. It never creates free capacity, removes cost or supplies a generic chain-revenue multiplier; it activates only when the Gus is executed at least Normally.
- Venue Fit is determined only by Landscape Connection, Building Character & Scale, Physical Program Facilities, Flow Fit, Local Market Fit and Visible Promise. Every compatible location + building combination must have two or three realistic Rare/Iconic Venue Fit routes at comparable investment levels; this is a required later balance test.
- Venue Fit now uses one canonical six-family data contract. Locations, building bases and upgrades contribute only their physically credible evidence tags; no upgrade gives a generic Venue Fit multiplier. Every offer must declare its facility tags, market direction, visible clue pool, eligible routes and one viable low-investment route before implementation or final art begins.
- Each venue has six hidden local-market directions: routine access, recovery interest, social/program interest, destination potential, premium acceptance and value sensitivity, plus daypart rhythm, seasonal pressure and arrival reach. Players learn these through truthful property clues, visible behaviour, individual guest cards, weekly diagnostics and Steam Guide/local mentions, never exposed scores or demographic shortcuts. Local demand can change only slowly through earned reach, return habit and concept association.
- Named guest memory is limited to a small, venue-local regular pool. Regulars keep a stable identity and at most three factual memories: a repeat anchor, a program/facility highlight or a concrete friction. They may become curious, occasional, regular, cooling or lapsed based on real opening hours, offer, value and capacity, but are never collectible VIPs, hard-scheduled characters or a source of demographic stereotypes.
- Trends and novelty are now separate bounded systems. Repetition removes only additional curiosity/word-of-mouth opportunity, never a program's viable baseline or Composition Tier. A slow interest shift is hinted at least one game week before it matters, remains legible for several weeks, gives more than one credible response and never forces a venue, item or program to become obsolete.
- A full game year lasts 52 real days. Seasons create bounded programme/guest-context opportunities and later ambient visual layers, but never close natural-water routes, gate materials, impose weather penalties or override the player's opening schedule. All authored water-entry routes remain available year-round with an appropriate maintained winter base state.
- A venue offer is a reviewable content unit: compatible location + authored plot + permanent building base + market/economy variation. Before implementation it must declare truthful property clues, physical capacity, facilities, available upgrades, market profile, low/high-fit routes, economic pressure and future route commitment. The test prototype starts at Canal + Repair Workshop; the production game later begins with three affordable viable offers.
- The first Program Library uses editable, visible-from-start Gus presets to teach composition and physical requirements. Presets never lock free composition, promise a tier or reserve a material for a location. Missing physical support is shown as a specific greyed-out requirement, and an edited version is simply a new Unproven composition.
- Cross-venue balance compares modest, focused, developed, ambitious and stressed routes for every building family. It must test water/non-water, small/large programs, low-cost/premium venues, staff automation and debt without allowing any one location, capacity size, upgrade or chain metric to become a universal answer.
- Ingredients, delivery forms, music, performance styles, Masters, equipment, venues and upgrades use one shared plain-language hint system: Calm, Fresh, Grounded, Warm, Ritual, Social, Rhythmic, Bold, Playful and Ceremonial. Player-facing teaching is short, varied descriptive card copy and contextual Good to know sentences, not a live compatibility map, tag arithmetic or recipe list. It should help players learn relationships without revealing final composition tier formulas or exact Ultimate recipes.
