# Sauna Sim Aufguss Program Core Card Copy v0.1

## Purpose

This is the English player-facing copy for the program builder's core choices: intent, heat profile, base format and highlighted recovery finish. It deliberately explains a choice's operational consequence as well as its atmosphere. Nothing here is a hard creative lock unless a visible facility is genuinely required.

## Intents

### Classic Ritual

**About**  
A clear sauna tradition: purposeful heat, recognisable craft and no unnecessary decoration. The program succeeds when each round feels like part of the same ritual.

**Good to know**  
Build it around woods, herbs, towelwork, vihta or natural water. A surprising material can still work, but give it a clear place in the sequence rather than adding it only for novelty.

### Quiet Recovery

**About**  
A lower-pressure session built around space, measured heat and a calm landing afterward. The point is not weak heat; it is a program that knows when not to rush.

**Good to know**  
Use Gentle Build, steady aromas, quieter music and a rest, shower or water finish. Avoid filling every round with sharp changes, loud music or a finale that breaks the promise of recovery.

### Social Energy

**About**  
A shared, welcoming session with a clear pulse and an easy reason for guests to join. Social Energy can be playful or premium, but it should never feel private or overly severe.

**Good to know**  
Use accessible music, citrus, warm notes, rhythmic towelwork or a refreshment finish. Keep the price and session length believable for a group program; an expensive, demanding Gus needs enough visible value to earn its place.

### Show Journey

**About**  
A program designed to develop through distinct moments. Heat, scent, music and performance should give guests a sense that the session is going somewhere.

**Good to know**  
Use Progressive Rounds, ordered materials, a capable Master and enough derived room time for an ending to land. Avoid choosing a large show promise for a Short format or a room that cannot support the audience and movement.

## Heat Profiles

### Gentle Build

**About**  
Heat rises gradually, leaving room for scent and recovery to register before the session reaches its warmer point.

**Good to know**  
Use it for Quiet Recovery, fresh or soft materials and guests who value a more approachable entry. Do not make the final round suddenly extreme without a visible bridge in the music or performance.

### Steady Heat

**About**  
A consistent temperature path with controlled rounds and few surprises. Steady Heat gives the Master a reliable base for a traditional or repeatable program.

**Good to know**  
Use it with Classic Towelwork, woods, herbs and programs that need dependable frequency. Avoid stacking too many dramatic delivery forms; the session will stop feeling steady.

### Progressive Rounds

**About**  
Each round adds a little more heat, presence or complexity than the last. Progressive Rounds give a longer Gus a clear shape without requiring a theatrical show.

**Good to know**  
Use ordered aroma rounds, a musical build or a recovery finish that feels earned. Start with a material that leaves somewhere to go; beginning at full intensity makes the later rounds feel flat.

### Rhythmic Pulses

**About**  
Heat arrives in deliberate waves that the Master can time to movement and music. The room feels active without needing to become chaotic.

**Good to know**  
Use it with Rhythmic Flow, a clear beat and a Master with solid Heat Craft. Avoid very still music and unrelated delicate rounds; the pulses need something the guests can recognise and follow.

### High-Heat Finale

**About**  
The strongest room-wide heat arrives as a deliberate closing moment. It is a finish, not a shortcut for making every Gus feel important.

**Good to know**  
Use it late in a program with enough room time, Heat Craft and a credible cooling or recovery route. Do not put it in a short, crowded session with no landing afterward; guests will remember pressure rather than a satisfying finish.

## Base Formats

### Short

**About**  
A compact session with one clear idea. Short programs protect schedule capacity and can still feel complete when they do not try to contain everything.

**Good to know**  
Use one or two focused rounds and a simple performance language. Avoid a complex Show Journey, several long delivery pauses or a demanding finale; the program will have no time to develop.

### Standard

**About**  
A balanced starting frame for a complete Gus. Standard gives enough time for a beginning, a main moment and a clear exit without tying up the room all day.

**Good to know**  
Use it for most Classic Ritual, Quiet Recovery and Social Energy programs. It can support a strong finale or recovery finish, but every added phase still reduces possible session frequency.

### Long

**About**  
An extended format for a program with genuine development. Long sessions can justify premium materials and a richer journey, but they ask more of the Master, room and daily schedule.

**Good to know**  
Use it for layered Show Journeys, carefully paced rituals or a premium destination session. Do not schedule it at high frequency unless the venue has the room, staff and recovery capacity to carry it.

## Highlighted Recovery Finishes

### No Added Finish

**About**  
The Gus ends at the sauna door and guests continue their ordinary visit. This keeps the program simple and leaves recovery choices open.

**Good to know**  
Use it when the core sauna session is the complete idea or when the venue has limited recovery capacity. A demanding High-Heat Finale may feel unfinished without a more deliberate way to cool down.

### Cold Plunge

**About**  
A deliberate heat-to-cold transition using a visible cold-water tub or pool. It gives a strong program a clear physical ending.

**Good to know**  
Use it after bold heat, rhythmic energy or an outdoor ritual when the plunge can handle the guests arriving together. Do not advertise it as a major finish if the facility is too small for the program's attendance.

### Natural Water Dip

**About**  
A return to the sea, lake or canal through an approved visible water route. Natural Water Dip makes the surroundings part of the program rather than only the background.

**Good to know**  
Use it with water-side venues, birch or coastal materials and a program that leaves guests enough time to make the transition. It needs a real safe access route; a water view alone cannot deliver this finish.

### Outdoor Shower

**About**  
A quick, practical cooling step that keeps the guests moving after the sauna. It is smaller in scale than a plunge but can make a compact Gus feel more complete.

**Good to know**  
Use it for short programs, busy venues or a simple outdoor transition. Do not treat it as an equal substitute for a destination water ritual in a premium program; it serves a different promise.

### Rest Deck

**About**  
A quieter finish where the guest can sit, cool down and let the session settle. Rest Deck rewards pacing and gives a longer Gus a calm final note.

**Good to know**  
Use it with Quiet Recovery, warm woods, Ambient, Soul & Jazz or a carefully paced ritual. Protect enough seats and route space when sessions finish together, or the calm ending becomes a queue.

### Refreshment Finish

**About**  
A visible water, tea or shop-service point after the session. Refreshment Finish gives a social or accessible Gus a natural point to continue the visit.

**Good to know**  
Use it with Social Energy, daytime programs or a venue whose service point can carry the extra guests. It helps value feel tangible, but it cannot rescue an expensive Gus whose sauna experience was weak.

## Copy Review Rules

- Recovery cards name the physical facility needed when the player does not own it yet.
- Heat and format copy must mention time, capacity or execution whenever that is the real trade-off.
- No card may make safety, guest preference or a creative pairing sound absolute when the system only models a tendency.
